## The Big Question

Both are LG OLEDs, both are superb for gaming — but the **LG G4 OLED TV** and the **LG 45GR95QE-B OLED Ultrawide** monitor are built for different rooms and different rigs. One is a big-screen living-room TV; the other is a desk-dominating curved ultrawide. If you're choosing an OLED for gaming, the question isn't which is better in absolute terms — it's where you play and what you play on. Here's the comparison.

## Spec Comparison

| Spec | LG G4 OLED TV | LG 45GR95QE-B OLED Ultrawide |
|---|---|---|
| Type | Flat OLED TV | Curved OLED monitor |
| Size | 55"–83" | 45" |
| Aspect ratio | 16:9 | 21:9 (ultrawide) |
| Resolution | 4K | 3440×1440 (UWQHD) |
| Refresh | 144Hz VRR | 240Hz (some sizes) |
| Response | Near-instant (OLED) | 0.03ms (OLED) |
| Best for | Console + couch, cinematic | Desk PC gaming, productivity |
| Curvature | Flat | Curved (immersion) |
| Price | $1,499+ (55") | $1,299+ |
| GeekTechReview Rating | 9.6 | 9.0 |

## Performance Comparison

Both deliver perfect OLED blacks, infinite contrast, and near-instant pixel response — so motion clarity and HDR are reference-grade on either. The difference is in the experience:

The **G4 TV** gives you sheer size and 4K resolution at 16:9 — ideal for console gaming (PS5, Xbox), cinematic single-player games, and any couch-based play. At 55–83 inches it fills your field of view and dominates a living room.

The **45GR95QE-B ultrawide** gives you immersion and speed at a desk. The 21:9 aspect ratio wraps around your vision for PC games that support it, and higher refresh rates (up to 240Hz) suit competitive PC gaming. It's also a productivity monster — the ultrawide replaces a multi-monitor setup for work.

## Immersion & Use Case

The TV wins for scale and shared/couch gaming; the ultrawide wins for desk immersion and the wrap-around effect that competitive and sim players love. The ultrawide's curve is genuinely engaging for solo PC play; the TV's flat panel is better for off-axis viewing with friends.

## Productivity

The ultrawide doubles as an outstanding work display — enough horizontal space for side-by-side windows without a second monitor. The TV is a poor desktop monitor (too big, wrong pixel density up close) but unbeatable as a media screen. If your screen pulls double duty for work and play at a desk, the ultrawide is the clear pick.

## Value Comparison

Pricing is comparable at entry sizes. The TV is the better value if you want a do-everything living-room screen (gaming, movies, sports); the ultrawide is the better value as a single desk display for both serious PC gaming and productivity. Both earned strong scores — the G4 TV a 9.6, the ultrawide a 9.0.

## The Verdict

**Tie — different rooms, different winners.** Choose the **LG G4 OLED TV** for living-room, couch, and console/cinematic gaming where size and shared viewing matter. Choose the **LG 45GR95QE-B OLED Ultrawide** for desk-based PC gaming and a productivity display where immersion and refresh rate matter. The G4's higher 9.6 score reflects that it's the best-in-class TV; the ultrawide's 9.0 reflects it's the best-in-class curved OLED monitor. Buy for where you actually sit.
