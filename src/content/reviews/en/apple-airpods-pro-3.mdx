The AirPods Pro 3 are the earbuds Apple should have shipped three years ago. They finally add USB-C, a meaningfully upgraded H3 chip, adaptive audio that's actually adaptive, and a legit clinical-grade hearing-aid mode. At $249 they're still the default recommendation for iPhone owners — and now they have fewer excuses to be.

## Design

Same stem-down silhouette, but the case is now IP54 dust/water resistant and the buds are IP54 too (up from IPX4 on the Pro 2). The stem is slightly shorter. Force-sensor controls return, with new "nod" gestures for Siri without saying "Hey Siri." The case finally has a built-in speaker for Find My chirps and a lanyard loop — small, long-overdue details.

## Sound and ANC

The H3 chip and new custom driver push a richer low-end and crisper treble than the Pro 2. Adaptive EQ runs 48,000 times per second. Sound signature is still consumer-friendly V-shaped, but cleaner. ANC is improved ~2x over Pro 2 per Apple's claim, and in practice it's a noticeable bump — subway noise drops to a murmur. Transparency mode with "Conversation Awareness" auto-lowers media when you speak, and it's the most natural transparency on any earbud.

Adaptive Audio mode blends ANC and transparency based on environment — it now transitions more smoothly than the Pro 2's occasionally janky shifts.

## Battery

Up to 6 hours with ANC on, 30 hours with the case. That's a real bump from the Pro 2's 6/30. USB-C charging, plus Qi2 wireless and a new MagSafe attachment. Fast charge: 5 minutes = 1 hour.

## The hearing-aid feature

This is the surprising headline. The AirPods Pro 3 ship with a clinically validated, FDA-cleared over-the-counter hearing-aid mode. You run a self-administered hearing test in iOS, and the AirPods apply a per-frequency gain profile. For mild-to-moderate hearing loss, this is a $249 alternative to a $3,000 hearing aid. It's a quietly significant accessibility story.

## What's wrong

- Still only Apple ecosystem. Android users need not apply.
- The fit is the same open-seal silicone tip design — if your ear canals reject it (they reject a lot of people), no ear-tip will save you.
- No lossless audio, no LDAC. AAC is the ceiling.
- The stem's capacitive controls can be triggered by adjusting the bud in your ear.
- 6 hours is good, but competitors (Sony WF-1000XM5) hit 8–12.

## The verdict

If you're on iPhone, the AirPods Pro 3 are the earbuds to buy, full stop. The hearing-aid mode alone makes them remarkable. If you're an audiophile chasing lossless or on Android, look elsewhere. Apple made the boring, correct earbud again — and that's the highest compliment a mainstream product can get.
