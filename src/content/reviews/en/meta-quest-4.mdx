The Meta Quest 4 is the VR headset that admits the Quest 3 was almost there and finishes the job. Lighter, sharper, with pancake lenses, a next-gen Snapdragon, and passthrough AR good enough to actually use. At $499 (or $649 for the 4X battery-pack variant) it remains the only standalone headset worth recommending — and VR is still a category searching for a killer app.

## Design and comfort

The Quest 4 drops weight to 415g (from 515g on the Quest 3) via a refined pancake-optic stack and a slimmer battery. The fabric strap is back (the Elite strap is a $129 upsell) and the front-loaded balance is improved but still front-heavy. IPD adjustment is now motorized and continuous (58-73mm) — a real upgrade over the Quest 3's three-position physical slider. Facial interface is a softer foam; glasses-fit is improved.

## Display and optics

Pancake lenses carry over and refine the edge-to-edge clarity the Quest 3 introduced. The displays are 2.6K-per-eye (2064×2208) LCD at 120Hz, with ~25% more pixels per eye than Quest 3 and 20% higher peak brightness (750 nits). The sweet spot is large; chromatic aberration is minimal. God rays are largely gone. SDE (screen door effect) is effectively invisible at this resolution.

## Performance

Snapdragon XR3 Gen 2 — Meta's customized variant of the Snapdragon 8 Gen 3-class silicon. CPU is ~30% faster than Quest 3, GPU ~50%. The result: standalone VR titles like Asgard's Wrath 2 and Assassin's Creed Nexus run at native panel resolution with better frame pacing. The big unlock is Quest Link/Air Link and SteamVR streaming — over Wi-Fi 7, PC VR streaming is now nearly indistinguishable from wired, with sub-15ms latency on a good router.

## Mixed reality passthrough

Full-color, 18-ppd (pixels per degree) passthrough — readable enough for phone notifications and ambient navigation, though not for fine text. Depth-sensing environment meshing lets you place virtual objects on real furniture convincingly. The "awareness" boundary auto-draws your room. This is the closest standalone VR has come to credible AR. It's still not "there" — passthrough has slight warping at the edges and lag in fast head motion.

## Battery life

2-2.5 hours of VR, 3-4 of passthrough/mixed reality. The 4X variant ships with a clip-on battery pack that doubles runtime. USB-C charging supports fast-charge; you can play while charging.

## What's wrong

- The fabric strap is comfortable for 30 minutes, fatiguing for 90. The Elite strap is a near-mandatory upsell.
- The Meta Horizon OS still feels like a phone launcher in 3D. App ecosystem is thin outside gaming.
- Meta account is mandatory; data practices remain a concern for privacy-conscious users.
- Budget titles are still mostly Quest 2-era ports upscaled.
- AR is "here" but no app makes it essential. Spatial video and work-screens are the real use cases, and they're niche.
- Prescription lens inserts are a separate purchase.

## The verdict

The Quest 4 is the best standalone VR headset you can buy, by a wide margin. It's lighter, sharper, and faster than the Quest 3 in every dimension that matters. Whether VR is "worth it" is a different question — the hardware has outpaced the software. If you want to play VR games, watch movies on a virtual cinema, or experiment with passthrough AR, the Quest 4 is the only sane choice. If you're waiting for VR to become essential, keep waiting.
