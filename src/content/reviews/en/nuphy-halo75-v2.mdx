NuPhy's Halo75 V2 is the keyboard for people who want their desk to look like a synthwave album cover and still type like a grown-up. It's a 75% gasket-mounted board with a 1.47-inch customization display, tri-mode wireless, and a typing sound that'll make your coworkers ask what you're typing on.

## Build and design

The Halo75 V2 uses a CNC aluminum case with a translucent dual-layered acrylic bottom — yes, it glows through the case. The integrated wrist rest is a polarizing choice; I found it comfortable, but it means the board isn't flat-bottomed for arbitrary placement. The 1.47-inch TFT screen sits top-center and can show time, battery, GIFs, or custom animations. It is, admittedly, silly. I love it.

## Gasket mounting and sound

The V2 refines the gasket structure over the original Halo75 — the plate now floats on silicone gaskets that give a bouncier, deeper compression. Combined with the dense foam layers and the stock PBT keycaps, the typing sound is deep and thocky without being hollow. It's one of the best-sounding out-of-the-box boards under $200.

## Connectivity

Tri-mode: Bluetooth 5.0 (up to 4 devices), 2.4GHz with a magnetic-stow dongle, and USB-C wired. The standout is the 8000mAh battery — rated for up to 240 days with RGB off over Bluetooth. That's not a typo. In my testing, a week of daily use with RGB on dropped the battery maybe 15%.

## Switches and stabilizers

I tested with the Moss switch (linear, factory-lubed, 50gf). Smooth, no scratch, light spring. NuPhy's stabilizers are well-tuned out of the box — minimal rattle, no wire ping. Hot-swap is supported (5-pin). The PBT dye-sublimation keycaps are doubleshot-adjacent in durability and resist shine well.

## The annoyances

- The screen's companion software (NuPhy Console) is fine but not as capable as VIA — no full remapping, limited macro support. You can flash QMK via community firmware, but that's a project.
- The wrist rest is non-removable without tools and polarizing.
- Per-key RGB is bright but the translucent case can look busy with saturated palettes.
- 2.4GHz polling is 1000Hz; Bluetooth is ~90-125Hz. Fine for typing.

## The verdict

The Halo75 V2 is the board to buy if you care about how your desk looks and sounds as much as how it types. It's not the bare-bones-modders'-paradise that the Q1 Pro is, but it has more personality than any board at this price. If you want quiet productivity, look elsewhere. If you want a board that makes you smile when you sit down, this is it.
