The Framework Laptop 16 is the laptop for people who print their own replacement parts and think soldered RAM is a moral failing. It's a 16-inch modular laptop with a hot-swappable GPU, swappable keyboard (mechanical or membrane), and an expansion-card system that replaces fixed ports. It is the most repairable laptop on the market. It is also a series of compromises you have to want.

## The modularity

The Expansion Bay (GPU) accepts a Radeon RX 7700S module that slides in from the side — no disassembly required. It's a clever mechanical system with a PCIe connector rated for hot-swap. The Expansion Card system on the sides lets you reconfigure ports: USB-C, USB-A, HDMI, DisplayPort, microSD, SSD storage, even a hotkey pad. Six slots total. The Input Deck is magnetic and swappable — pick low-profile membrane, or a per-key-illuminated mechanical keyboard with hot-swap switches. You can also place a numpad or macro pad in different layouts.

This is the only laptop where you can change the GPU, the keyboard, the ports, and the storage architecture without tools.

## Performance

CPU is the AMD Ryzen 7 8040HS (8-core Zen 4, 5.1GHz). With the RX 7700S Expansion Bay module, you get real discrete graphics — 8GB GDDR6, ~12 TFLOPs. In Cyberpunk 2077 at 1600p medium, ~75fps native, ~110 with FSR. It's not a gaming laptop, but it games credibly. Without the GPU module (or in "light" mode with it removed for battery), it's a competent ultraportable.

Geekbench 6: 2,600 single, 12,000 multi. Cinebench R24 multi: ~1,400.

RAM and storage are user-replaceable — DDR5 SO-DIMM (up to 96GB) and two M.2 2280 NVMe slots. This alone makes it unlike every other laptop in 2026.

## Build and display

16-inch 2560×1600 IPS at 165Hz, 100% sRGB, 500 nits, matte. It's a good panel, not great — no OLED option, color accuracy is fine for work, not for color grading. Build is a magnesium-aluminum alloy, 1.9 kg (heavier without GPU). It flexes more than a MacBook under torsion, but feels durable.

Battery is 90Wh. With the GPU module out, expect 8-10 hours of light work. With it in and under load, 2 hours. The GPU module is a power cost.

## What's wrong

- The expansion modules add gaps and seams — it's not a unibody. Creaks slightly.
- The fan noise under load is louder than competitors.
- The Expansion Bay GPU is a generation behind and you're locked to AMD modules.
- No OLED display option.
- $1,399 base + $400 for the GPU module puts it in MacBook territory for less polish.
- Linux support is excellent (Framework ships a custom Fedora image); Windows support is fine but the AMD GPU driver switching occasionally hiccups.
- The keyboard deck can shift when carrying the open laptop.

## The verdict

The Framework Laptop 16 is not the best laptop at any single thing. It is the best laptop at being yours — repairable, upgradeable, reconfigurable, and free of planned obsolescence. If you value that ethos above benchmark peaks and unibody polish, it's the only laptop that matters. If you just want the best 16-inch laptop, buy a MacBook Pro.
