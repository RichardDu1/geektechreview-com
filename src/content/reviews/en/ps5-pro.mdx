The PlayStation 5 Pro is the mid-generation refresh that asks you to pay $699 for a console that doesn't include a disc drive or a vertical stand. It is unambiguously the most powerful console ever made. It is also the most expensive console launch Sony has attempted, and the value math is uncomfortable.

## The specs

Custom RDNA 3.5 GPU with 67% more compute units and 28% faster memory than the base PS5. The headline: 2-3x ray tracing throughput via dedicated "ray intersection engines" — RT now runs at a fraction of the cost, enabling more lights, reflections, and shadows without tanking framerate. CPU is the same 8-core Zen 2 (slightly higher-clocked) — the bottleneck is unchanged. 2TB SSD, 16GB GDDR6 + 2GB DDR5 for system tasks.

The other headline: PlayStation Spectral Super Resolution (PSSR), Sony's AI upscaling. It's Sony's DLSS — a neural network reconstructs lower-resolution frames to 4K with detail and antialiasing. It's good, though currently behind DLSS 4 in motion clarity and ghosting on fast-moving elements.

## Performance in practice

Games with "Pro Enhanced" patches see meaningful upgrades: locked 60fps modes that were unlocked/variable on base PS5, higher-resolution rendering with PSSR, and RT enabled where it wasn't before. In Spider-Man 2, the RT mode now runs 60fps at near-4K with full reflections. In Final Fantasy VII Rebirth, the Performance mode hits a locked 60 with much sharper image quality. The base PS5 versions of these same games are noticeably worse.

The catch: developers have to patch games. The supported list is growing but not exhaustive. Older or unsupported titles see no benefit.

## The disc drive situation

The PS5 Pro is digital-only out of the box. The optional disc drive is $79.99 and attaches externally. If you have a PS4/PS5 physical library, you're paying $780 minimum. The vertical stand — previously included — is another $29.99. The death-by-a-thousand-upsells is the genuinely cynical part of this launch.

## What's good

- Best console image quality and framerate stability available.
- PSSR is a credible upscaler that will improve with patches.
- 2TB SSD is generous (base PS5 is 1TB).
- Whisper-quiet; the fan is large and low-RPM.
- Backward-compatible with the entire PS4/PS5 library.

## What's wrong

- $699 with no disc drive and no stand. The "Pro" tax is steep.
- CPU is unchanged — games bottlenecked by CPU (some open-world titles) see minimal improvement.
- PSSR is behind DLSS 4 in motion, and some early patches have visible artifacts.
- The benefit is only realized on patched titles; your existing library may not see upgrades.
- No 8K output despite marketing — 8K is a checkbox, not a real target.
- It's a mid-gen refresh in a generation that hasn't peaked — most players haven't exhausted their base PS5.
- The PS5 Slim is $399. The Pro is nearly double for incremental gains.

## The verdict

The PS5 Pro is the most powerful console ever made and the worst value in console gaming. If you play on a high-end 4K/120Hz OLED and want the best image quality and stable 60fps, the Pro delivers — visibly, measurably. If you're a casual player or on a base PS5, the upgrade is hard to justify. Sony built a Ferrari for a console generation still figuring out whether it needs one. It's excellent hardware sold at a price that dares you to say no.
