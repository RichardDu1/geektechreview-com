The Sony WH-1000XM6 is the headphone that admits the XM5 was a mistake and course-corrects hard. It folds again, it cancels noise better, and it costs $449. Sony's flagship has returned to the top of the ANC heap.

## Design — the fold is back

The XM5 abandoned the folding hinge for a sleeker look and made every traveler furious. The XM6 brings the fold back. The headband is slimmer, the earcups are slightly smaller, and the whole thing collapses into a case that actually fits in a backpack pocket. Weight is 250g — lighter than the XM5 and barely heavier than the Bose QC Ultra. Materials are matte plastic and protein leather; they don't feel $449-premium in the hand, but the build is solid.

## Active noise cancellation

This is the headline. Sony's new Integrated Processor V2 plus an 8-mic array pushes ANC performance past the Bose QC Ultra and Apple AirPods Max. On a transcontinental flight, engine roar is reduced to a faint hum — the cabin feels pressurized. Low-frequency attenuation is best-in-class, and mid-range (voices, PA systems) is meaningfully better than the XM5. Sony's adaptive ANC now reacts in real-time to environment changes without the whistling/pumping artifacts older adaptive modes had.

## Sound

The XM6 uses the same 30mm carbon-fiber driver as the XM5 but with a refined DSP. Out of the box it's still slightly V-shaped — boosted bass, smoothed treble — but less aggressively so than the XM5. With LDAC support (990 kbps) over Bluetooth 5.3 and Sony's DSEE Extreme upscaling, lossy streams sound richer. Wired USB-C lossless is supported. Purists will still want EQ; the Sony Headphones Connect app offers a proper 5-band plus presets.

## Battery and features

- 30 hours with ANC on (40 with ANC off). Fast charge: 3 minutes = 3 hours.
- Multipoint Bluetooth that actually works — no manual device switching.
- Speak-to-Chat, auto-pause when removed, wear detection — all present, all optional.
- LDAC, AAC, SBC codecs. No aptX Lossless (Sony reserves that for its own ecosystem).

## What's still annoying

- $449 is a lot for plastic. The case is included at least.
- Touch controls are capacitive and gesture-based — fine, but cold-weather glove users suffer.
- The Sony Headphones Connect app is bloated and asks for location data for no defensible reason.
- No 3.5mm aux in the box — USB-C to 3.5mm cable only.

## The verdict

The XM6 is the best all-around wireless headphone you can buy in 2026. If you fly, it's a no-brainer. If you want reference sound at home, buy open-backs. The XM6's job is to make the world disappear — and it does that better than anything else on the shelf.
