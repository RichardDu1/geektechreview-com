The DJI Mini 4 Pro is the drone that makes "I don't need a drone" the hardest argument to win. At 249g it's under the FAA's 250g registration threshold, yet it packs omnidirectional obstacle sensing, a 4K/100fps 1/1.3-inch sensor, and 34-minute flight time. It is the best consumer drone under $1,000, and it's not close.

## The camera

The 1/1.3-inch CMOS sensor shoots 4K/100fps (great for slow-mo), 48MP stills, and supports 10-bit Dlog-M and HLG for color grading. HDR video stacks frames for better dynamic range. The f/1.7 aperture is bright enough for usable dusk footage. Night mode stacks long exposures for low-light stills. Is it a Mirrorless-killer? No. Does it outclass every phone gimbal combo? Comfortably.

True vertical shooting — the camera physically rotates 90° — is a TikTok/Reels godsend. No more cropping landscape footage into a phone aspect ratio.

## Flight performance

O3+ transmission gives 20km range (legal line-of-sight limits aside) and 1080p/30 live feed. Omnidirectional binocular vision means APAS 5.0 will route around obstacles in real-time. Return-to-home is reliable. Wind resistance is 10.7 m/s (Level 5), and I've flown it in 15 mph gusts without incident. Flight time is a real 30-34 minutes per battery in mild conditions.

## Intelligence

ActiveTrack 360 with omnidirectional sensing means the drone can follow you around, in front, behind, or circling, and not fly into a tree. Spotlight 2.0 locks the camera on a subject while you fly freely. MasterShots auto-generates cinematic sequences with music. Cruise Control holds a direction hands-free.

## Build and portability

Folds to 138×81×58mm — fits in a jacket pocket. Weighs 249g with the standard battery. The "Plus Battery" pushes flight time to 45 minutes but tips the drone over 250g (you'll need to register). Build quality is the expected DJI premium plastic.

## What's wrong

- The 1/1.3-inch sensor can't match the Mavic 3's 4/3 sensor for low light or shallow depth.
- Obstacle sensing struggles with thin branches and power lines — don't trust it blindly.
- The included RC-N2 controller needs your phone; upgrade to the DJI RC 2 with built-in screen ($200 more) for a real flying experience.
- DJI's app pushes firmware updates and account login aggressively.
- Care Refresh insurance is worth it; drone crashes are inevitable.

## The verdict

The Mini 4 Pro is the drone that killed the "drone as a niche gadget" argument. It's small enough to carry everywhere, smart enough to fly itself, and shoots video good enough for client work. If you've ever wanted a drone, this is the one. Register it (or don't, at 249g), get Care Refresh, and go fly.
