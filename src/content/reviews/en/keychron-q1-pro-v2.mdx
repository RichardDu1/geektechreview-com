The Keychron Q1 Pro is what happens when a company decides to build the "first real mechanical keyboard" for people who aren't ready to spend $400 on a barebones boutique board. It's a gasket-mounted, CNC-machined aluminum keyboard with QMK/VIA support and tri-mode wireless, and it starts at $179.

## The build

The case is a solid billet of CNC aluminum weighing 1.6 kg — this keyboard does not move when you type. Gasket mounting uses poron strips between plate and case, giving a soft, bouncy typing feel that's a night-and-day shift from tray-mount boards. The plate is steel (stock) or FR4/CNC aluminum if you buy the barebones variant. Double gasket structure adds damping under the PCB.

## Connectivity

Tri-mode: Bluetooth 5.1 (three-device pairing), 2.4GHz dongle, and USB-C wired. Bluetooth is rock-solid — no dropouts across a week of daily use. Switching between paired devices is a two-key combo. USB-C supports N-key rollover and 1000Hz polling; over 2.4GHz you get 1000Hz, over Bluetooth ~125Hz (fine for typing, not for competitive gaming).

## QMK and VIA

Full QMK firmware with VIA support means you can remap every key, set layers, and configure macros in a browser tab — no flashing gymnastics. Knob versions add an encoder that you can map to volume, scroll, or anything QMK can express. Hot-swap is standard, PCB-mount and plate-mount stab support.

## Typing feel

Out of the box with the stock plate and pre-lubed switches (I tested with banana tactile), the Q1 Pro sounds thick and muted — no ping, minimal rattle. Stabilizers arrive lubed and well-tuned, which is not something you can say about most sub-$200 boards. The gasket give is moderate — not mushy, not rigid.

## What I'd change

- The stock keycaps are fine but not doubleshot PBT on all layouts — buy a set if you care.
- Battery is 4000mAh; rated ~270 hours RGB-off over Bluetooth. Fine, not exceptional.
- The 2.4GHz dongle storage is a magnetic compartment — clever but easy to lose.
- No per-key RGB dimming independent of brightness without VIA digging.

## The verdict

The Q1 Pro is the board I recommend to anyone asking "what's the first real keyboard I should buy." It undercuts boutique boards by $200 and loses almost nothing. If you want to dip into the hobby without falling down the $600 rabbit hole on day one, start here.
