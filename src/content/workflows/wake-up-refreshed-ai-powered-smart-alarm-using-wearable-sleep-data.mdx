## Overview

Waking up during deep sleep leaves you feeling groggy and disoriented. By using your wearable's sleep phase data, an AI alarm can wake you during light sleep, making mornings easier. This guide uses Sleep as Android (or similar), IFTTT, and a smart speaker to create a seamless wake-up experience.

## Prerequisites

- A wearable that tracks sleep stages (e.g., Fitbit, Garmin, Oura Ring, or Apple Watch)
- Sleep as Android app (or iOS alternative like AutoSleep)
- IFTTT account (free tier works)
- Smart speaker or smart lights (optional for gradual wake-up)

## Step 1: Set Up Sleep Tracking on Your Wearable

Ensure your wearable syncs sleep data to a compatible app. For this guide, we'll use Sleep as Android because it integrates with many wearables and has a built-in smart alarm.

1. Install Sleep as Android on your phone.
2. Pair your wearable via the app's settings (e.g., Wear OS, Fitbit, Garmin).
3. Grant necessary permissions for sleep data access.
4. Test by wearing the device to bed for one night to confirm data syncs.

## Step 2: Configure the Smart Alarm in Sleep as Android

Sleep as Android's smart alarm uses accelerometer and heart rate data to detect sleep phases.

1. Open Sleep as Android > Settings > Alarm.
2. Enable "Smart Period" and set it to 30 minutes (the alarm will wake you within this window before your set time).
3. Choose a gradual alarm sound (e.g., nature sounds) to ease you awake.
4. Set your desired final wake time (e.g., 7:00 AM).
5. Optional: Enable CAPTCHA to prevent snoozing.

## Step 3: Connect to IFTTT for Smart Home Actions

IFTTT can trigger smart lights or speakers when the alarm goes off.

1. Create an IFTTT account and install the app.
2. Connect Sleep as Android to IFTTT (use the Sleep as Android channel).
3. Create an applet: "If Sleep as Android alarm goes off, then turn on smart lights" (e.g., Philips Hue).
4. Alternatively, use Google Home or Alexa to play a morning playlist.

## Step 4: Test the System

1. Simulate a sleep session by manually starting a sleep tracking in Sleep as Android.
2. Set a test alarm 5 minutes later.
3. Confirm the alarm triggers during light sleep (the app shows sleep phases).
4. Verify IFTTT actions fire correctly.

## Step 5: Fine-Tune and Optimize

- Adjust the smart period window (15-45 minutes) based on your sleep cycle length.
- Experiment with different alarm sounds and light colors.
- If using multiple wearables, ensure only one is the primary source.

## Troubleshooting

- **No sleep data**: Check wearable sync and app permissions.
- **Alarm not smart**: Ensure smart period is enabled and wearable is worn correctly.
- **IFTTT not triggering**: Re-link accounts and check applet status.

## Conclusion

With this setup, you'll wake up during light sleep, feeling more refreshed. The combination of wearable data, AI alarm, and smart home integration creates a personalized wake-up experience. Sweet dreams and good mornings!
